# clear-ops

The agentic workforce for Clear Exchange, operated by the founder from Telegram, Discord or WhatsApp, any or all at once.

## What this is
A small Node service. Messages arrive from Telegram (long polling, `src/telegram.ts`), Discord (gateway, `src/discord.ts`; every job Paul starts gets its own thread: under his message for the first job from it, otherwise a standalone thread in the text channel, including batches and jobs asked for inside a thread; follow-on jobs report in the original job's thread; threads auto-archive after a week and a report reopens one; a thread that fails to open is logged and the job reports in the conversation) or WhatsApp (`/webhook`), and replies go back to the conversation they came from through `src/channels.ts`. Jobs interrupted by a restart are re-queued on boot and carry on their own Agent SDK conversation (`Job.sessionId`, reported early by `RunOptions.onSession`; sessions live under `DATA_DIR/claude`; resumed with `continueNote`, falling back to a fresh run with the usual resume note if the session cannot be resumed; a retry or `/resume` starts fresh). Deploys wait for quiet: `.github/workflows/deploy.yml`'s `wait-for-quiet` job polls `GET /busy` (`JobRunner.activeCount`, a count only) until no worker runs, up to 45 minutes, and a newer merge cancels an older waiting run so close merges go out as one deploy. Every job's thread gets a running commentary once it has run a minute: one message edited in place every 20 seconds with the worker's latest step and the elapsed time (`JobProgress` and `describeWorkerTool` in `src/status.ts`, plain words only, never a command), removed before the job's finished message; channels that cannot edit get nothing. Every job has a title (`src/titles.ts`): five words or fewer, chosen by the chief in `start_job` and `schedule_job` (validated), or derived for jobs the system starts ("Review: X", "Re-review 1: X", "Fix round 2: X", "Design round 2: X", "Build round 1: X", a scheduled run takes its schedule's title, "Draft nudge <id>"). The title names the Discord thread and is what every message, `/jobs`, `job_status` and queue line shows (`Fly deploy · engineer · 16d5d3`); the brief stays on the job unchanged for the worker. Jobs and schedules stored before titles get a derived one on load. a chief-of-staff agent (Claude Agent SDK) replies, and delegates longer work to worker agents (research, write, engineer, maintain, analyse, create_agent, ea, design, ideate, cv, dns, advisor, prebid) that run in the background and message the founder when done. Every agent can read every managed repository and the founder's uploads; every worker prompt names every checkout's local path (`repoPaths()` in `src/agents.ts`). Managed repositories (`src/repos.ts`, `repoRegistry`): `product` and `self` (PRODUCT_REPO, SELF_REPO; unchanged defaults and the targets of all older jobs), the `MANAGED_REPOS` list (default `site=drpaulfarrow/drpaulfarrowcom`, Paul's personal site, cloned under `DATA_DIR/repos/<alias>`), and repositories the workforce created, persisted in the store (`createdRepos`) and reloaded on boot. `Job.target` is a repository alias; `start_job` takes `repo` (alias, owner/name or Paul's name for it, default product) and `bootstrapRepos` (`src/bootstrap.ts`) clones each at start and fast-forwards an existing checkout to its default branch when it is on that branch with no local changes (`refreshCheckout`), so agents read the deployed code. Engineer and maintain work in the chosen checkout; research, write, analyse and design read it. A repository that cannot be cloned or created is reported to Paul once in plain English with exactly what to change in the token (`explainCloneFailure`, `explainCreateFailure`), and the token is redacted from every log and message (`redact`). New repositories: the `create_repo` permit (`src/repo-tools.ts`), which only an engineer job gets, only when the chief grants it because Paul asked in chat; it creates one private-by-default repository under `REPO_OWNER` with a README and initial commit (GitHub `auto_init`), registers and clones it, and nothing else (no deletion, visibility or settings change, no pushes). Research has a depth (`start_job` `depth`, or inferred from the brief by `detectDepth`): the saved report has no word cap and is sized to the brief, and a deep job writes a long, structured report with an executive summary, with higher turn and spend caps (`runLimits`, `DEEP_RESEARCH_BUDGET_USD`). Files he attaches on Telegram or Discord are saved by `src/uploads.ts` under `DATA_DIR/uploads` (`<date>-<name>`), confirmed in chat, and described to the chief in that turn (path, original name, type, size, caption); images are passed to the chief inline so it can see them. `/uploads`, `/delete upload <name>` and the chief's `list_uploads` and `delete_upload` manage them. The executive assistant (`ea`, `src/ea.ts`) works over Paul's Google Workspace at the level he approved, "drafts plus holds", widened on 2 Nov 2026 to sending and inviting with his per-item approval: it reads mail and every calendar, and only when the chief grants the job a permit from Paul's chat does it save Gmail drafts, place, move and remove holds on his own calendar, or (permit `send`) prepare an email or an invitation with attendees, which goes out only after Paul replies `send <id>` himself (`src/outbound.ts`, see the invariants). Paul connects once via a link from the chief (`/google/callback`); the refresh token is stored encrypted under `DATA_DIR/secrets`, and connecting schedules a weekday 07:30 brief and 18:00 wrap. The ea also keeps a follow-up list in the store (`src/followups.ts`: people waiting on Paul, his promises in sent mail, people he waits on, with due and stale dates), scanned in the morning brief, nudged in chat when stale, managed with `/followups`, `done <id>`, `snooze <id> <when>`, `draft nudge <id>`; drafts outreach with a Gmail link per draft; and proposes meetings (`propose_meeting`: two or three grouped holds plus Paul's booking link, `/booking`), and `HoldWatcher` in `src/meetings.ts` releases a group's holds once the meeting is confirmed on his calendar. Engineers implement on branches and open pull requests. One agent, the maintainer, reviews and merges; it runs automatically after every engineer job and every agent-creator job that may have opened a pull request. The agent-creator (`create_agent`) turns a plain-language request for a new agent into a recommended schedule on an existing kind or a new worker kind on a pull request against this repository. Up to `ENGINEER_SLOTS` engineering jobs (engineer or create_agent) run per managed repository at once (product, self, site and any other counted separately; `lockKey`), each in its own working copy: the first in the checkout, the others in git worktrees beside it (`laneDir`, `<checkout>.lane1`; `Job.lane`, kept across restarts; `ensureLane` creates one on the latest default branch and keeps unfinished work; a checkout that is not a git repository at all, a failed clone or a test fixture, is used directly as before, exactly as it was), and the engineer in a worktree is told to work only there (`laneNote`). The number is a plain setting (`engineerSlots` in `src/config.ts`): the default is `ENGINEER_SLOTS_DEFAULT` (2), the hard maximum is `ENGINEER_SLOTS_MAX` (8; Paul's call, 13 Nov 2026, more parallel work), the live value is 4 in `fly.toml`, and a bigger number is refused with the extra jobs queued exactly as before; the start-up log says which number is in force (`engineerSlotsLine`). Copy set-up for one checkout is serialised (`serialiseLaneSetup`) and retried once, so two jobs starting together cannot collide in git, and a worktree git will not make never means sharing one: that job waits for the checkout to be free and takes it as its own copy (`claimCheckout`: it holds its slot, is reported as waiting by the chief's `job_alive`, `checkLiveness` never calls it stalled while it waits, and `activeCount` counts it as not working, so a deploy does not wait on it). Further ones queue in FIFO order in the store (`waitingFor`, `queuePos`) and start when a slot frees, including across restarts. When a job starts while the machine is already at or above one runnable task per core (`loadNote`) or nearly out of memory, under 12% of it free (`readMemory`, `memoryNote`), one line goes to the log; nothing throttles, delays or reprioritises anything on it. The maintainer and other kinds never queue. The review loop closes itself: when the maintainer's review of an engineer's or agent-creator's pull request ends `REVIEW <url>: changes requested`, the job runner starts an engineer fix round on the same pull request (queued like any engineering job, re-reviewed as usual), up to `MAX_FIX_ROUNDS` (3); after that, or on `REVIEW <url>: needs Paul: ...`, the founder is messaged once with what he must do. Engineers and the maintainer update branches from main with `gh pr update-branch` or `git pull --no-rebase origin main`, never by force. The design worker (`design`) screenshots the live site and branches with headless Chromium (`src/design.ts`), critiques them as buyer personas and writes brand work and specs; it writes no production code. The creative strategist (`ideate`) has only WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob and Grep (no Edit, Write, Bash or secrets), reads the saved outputs and both repositories, and writes a ranked list of improvement ideas with impact, effort and the cheapest experiment; it only lists options, and neither it nor the chief starts an engineer, design, analyse or create_agent job from its ideas until Paul picks one in chat. The pitch and strategy advisor (`advisor`, `src/decks.ts`) has WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob, Grep and one tool of its own, `save_deck_file`, which writes Markdown or text only (slide-ready Marp Markdown, one-pagers, consult notes) into `DATA_DIR/decks` (owner-only, outside every checkout); it builds publisher, AI-company and investor pitches in Paul's voice and acts as a candid consultant from the saved research, never invents traction, customers, quotes or numbers (sourced or marked ASSUMPTION, simulated results labelled), has no mail, send, Bash or job tools, never starts jobs, and is one-off. The Prebid expert (`prebid`) has only WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob and Grep (no Edit, Write, Bash or secrets); it answers Prebid questions, designs and reviews the seller-side Prebid module, explains the Prebid codebases, contribution rules, release process and governance (CODEBASE mode, reading the forks under github.com/drpaulfarrow over the web where public) and watches releases from primary sources, states dates and versions, says "not found" rather than inventing fields or versions, never posts, files or messages anything, and starts no jobs. The CV writer (`cv`, `src/cv.ts`) has WebSearch, WebFetch (not LinkedIn), Read, Glob, Grep and one tool of its own, `save_cv_file`, which writes Markdown or text only into `DATA_DIR/cv` (owner-only, outside every checkout); it keeps a facts file, a master CV and bios, tailors them from facts Paul supplied, flags gaps as questions and possible Microsoft-confidential items for him, has no mail, send or job tools, and is one-off. The DNS agent (`dns`, `src/dns.ts`, `src/dns-gate.ts`, setup in `docs/GODADDY.md`) works over Paul's GoDaddy zones for the allowlisted `GODADDY_DOMAINS`: it has no Bash, Edit, Write or web tools, only its own `dns` server (read tools always; `dns_prepare_change` only for a job holding the `dns` permit, which the chief grants only because Paul asked in chat and scheduled runs never get). It only PREPARES: `DnsGate` reads the live zone, applies the hard blocks (no deleting, no NS, MX, SRV, CAA, SOA, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, apex A, AAAA or CNAME, no wildcards, at most 5 records, allowlisted domains only) and the service shows Paul the exact records before and after with an id; only his chat message `dns apply <id>` (whole message, id named, parsed by the orchestrator after the allowlist check and before any model; `dns cancel <id>`) applies it. Approvals expire (2 hours), a zone that changed since he saw it needs a new preparation, the whole zone is snapshotted before every applied change (`DATA_DIR/dns/snapshots`, owner-only) and every applied change is logged in the store and `dns/changes.log`. `GoDaddyClient.write` needs a single-use grant minted only in `src/dns-gate.ts`, and the client has no route to delete or to any registrar call (transfer, renew, contacts, nameservers). `GODADDY_API_TOKEN` (Bearer, Domains v3, wins when set) and `GODADDY_API_KEY` and `GODADDY_API_SECRET` (sso-key, v1 fallback) are service secrets, stripped from every worker's environment and scrubbed from errors and logs (`redact` too). Creating an account for Paul is the sign-up gate (`src/signup.ts`, `src/signup-gate.ts`, docs in `docs/SIGNUP.md`), the only route by which an account is ever made, and it is never autonomous. A job of a kind that carries the browser (`research`, `ideate`, `advisor`, `prebid`) with the `signup` permit, granted by the chief only because Paul asked in chat for an account on a site he named (scheduled runs never get it), reads the site's own sign-up page and calls `signup_prepare`; it may only PREPARE. The service, not the model, then applies the hard blocks and shows Paul the exact site, the address to use (`SIGNUP_EMAILS`; with none named every sign-up is refused), every field that would be filled in, a link to the terms and an id. The username is resolved once, in `prepare` (`resolveUsername`: the worker's own argument, else a field it labelled as a username, else the address's local part) and recorded on the action, so the preview, the hash, the page's username field and the vault all carry that one value. A required consent box ("I agree to the Terms of Service") is named in the plan with the value `yes` so Paul sees what he is accepting, is ticked then, and is refused outright if the plan does not name it. Only his own chat message `signup apply <id>` (`signup cancel <id>` cancels; whole message, id named, parsed by the orchestrator after the allowlist check and before any model) creates it: the service re-checks the plan against a hash of exactly what he saw, checks the day's cap again, generates the password itself, fills the form and submits it. The login goes into the vault (site, username, password) only when the page positively confirms the account (`SUCCESS_AFTER`: "welcome", "account created", "check your inbox" and the like); a captcha wall, a server error, "already registered" or a rejected form (`FAILURE_AFTER`, or no positive signal at all) is `unclear`: nothing is stored, Paul is told it could not be confirmed, and the site is not blocked as a duplicate. The password is never in chat, in a log or in a job output. The browser that submits is opened for the plan's own domain alone (`mayAct: hostMatches(host, domain)`, never widened by the act list, and only that domain's saved cookies, `cookiesFor`), so an untrusted sign-up page cannot reach anywhere else with Paul's cookies. Approvals expire (`SIGNUP_TTL_HOURS`, 6), there is a daily cap (`SIGNUP_DAILY_CAP`, 3) enforced in `prepare` and again in `approve`, the site must be one Paul has allowed (`src/site-policy.ts`) or on `SIGNUP_SITES`, a site with a login already in the vault is refused as a duplicate, `signup_prepare`'s `replaces` only ever cancels that same job's own waiting plan, and one JSON line per event (`prepared`, `refused` with its reason, `cancelled`, `stopped`, `applied`, `failed`) goes to `DATA_DIR/signup/actions.log` (owner-only, never the password). Fixed whatever anyone says: never pay or enter card, bank or billing details, never start a paid trial that needs them; never phone, identity, government or age verification; never a money site, email provider, Microsoft, password manager, government identity service or this workforce's own infrastructure; never a site Paul did not name; never a second account where a login is stored; never a change to an existing account or deleting one; never posting or publishing as him. Buying is the purchase gate (`src/purchase.ts`), the only place the workforce pays, and every purchase is approved by Paul one by one (no spending limit; his call, 13 Nov 2026). A job of a browse kind with the `purchase` permit, granted by the chief only because Paul asked in chat to buy something (scheduled runs never get it), fills the basket in its browser (signing in from the vault), may take checkout steps (`checkoutStepOnly`: "Proceed to checkout", "Continue to payment", "Use this payment method") but never press the button that places the order (`FINAL_ORDER`, refused to every agent), and calls `purchase_prepare` with that button's number. The service, not the model, reads the page (`BrowseSession.checkout`): shop, `orderTotal`, items, the button's words, whether it asks for card details, and a screenshot; refuses non-https pages, `NEVER_SITES`, a page with no readable total and a card checkout with no card stored; and sends Paul the shop, items and total with a one-time approval page (`/purchase/<id>?t=`, Approve or Decline, the screenshot, single-use token, strict CSP). Only his tap there or his own chat message `buy <id>` (`buy cancel <id>` declines; whole message, parsed by the orchestrator after the allowlist check and before any model) approves it; the job waits with it for `PURCHASE_WAIT_MINUTES` (20). Approved: `completePurchase` checks the page is still the same host showing the same total, enters the vault card only if the page asks for one (`CardVault.forApprovedPurchase`, read by `src/purchase.ts` alone), presses the button and lets the page write anywhere for 90 seconds only; changed means nothing is pressed. The card (`src/vault.ts` `CardVault`) is added only on the one-time `/vault/card` page (link from the chief's `vault_card_link`, 15 minutes), checked (Luhn, expiry, CVC), stored encrypted with `VAULT_KEY`, never shown to a model, in chat, in the store or in a log (only its last four digits), and agents' `type` refuses card fields. Every step is one JSON line in `DATA_DIR/purchases/actions.log`. The design loop (`start_job` with `loop: true`) closes itself the same way: a design report ending `DESIGN: spec ready` starts an engineer job on the product (queued and reviewed as usual); when the maintainer merges it, the next design round starts once the product's deploy is live; up to `MAX_DESIGN_ROUNDS` (3) implemented rounds, then a closing critique. `DESIGN: no high-impact issues` ends it early; `DESIGN: needs Paul: ...` pauses it, sends him the images, and `start_job` with `continueLoop` carries on. A worker run that ends in an API or SDK error rather than real work (no credit, a rejected key, rate limits or overload after retries, an execution error, an empty or error-only result; `runFailure` in `src/failures.ts`, fed by `RunResult.error` from `sdkRunner`, which reads the SDK's `is_error` results) is recorded as `failed` with `failure` and `error` set, never `done`; nothing follows it (no review, fix round, design step or "Finished"). A run stopped for money is one of those failures: the job's own cap counted in the provider's money, or the SDK's runaway guard counted in its own estimate, recorded as failure `budget`, with Paul told in one plain sentence what it really spent, what the cap was and which measure that was, and with no figure quoted at all when the provider's could not be read. `FailureNotifier` tells Paul in one plain message per burst (a short batch window, then one follow-up per cooldown, per reason). Credit and auth failures set the store's `queueHold`: no queued engineering job starts and new ones queue behind it, until `/resume`, the chief's `resume_jobs` or `retry <id>` (`JobRunner.resume` and `retry`) lifts it and reruns the failed jobs under the same ids. The chief's prompt says a failed job did no work, and names an active hold. Liveness (`src/jobs.ts`): every worker run has a heartbeat (`RunOptions.onActivity`, each SDK message; `lastActivityAt` and `startedAt` on the job) and an abort handle; `JobRunner.checkLiveness`, run every minute by `startWatchdog` once boot has resumed interrupted jobs, stops a job idle for `JOB_STALL_MINUTES` (30) or running past `JOB_MAX_RUNTIME_MINUTES` (240) as failed with failure `stalled` or `timeout`, stashes an engineering job's uncommitted work in its checkout (`stashWork`, recorded as `savedWork`, which a retry is told to apply), frees its lock so the next queued job starts, and tells Paul in one sentence; a worker that ignores the abort is left behind after a grace period. A job marked running with no live worker after boot is reclaimed like a restart (resumed, or stopped after three), and a free lock with waiters starts its queue. The chief's `job_alive` says whether a running job's worker is alive and when it last did anything, `cancel_job` (`JobRunner.cancel`, failure `cancelled`) stops it or takes it out of the queue and frees its slot, and `move_job_to_front` (`JobRunner.moveToFront`) reorders a repository's queue. A daily spend cap (`DAILY_BUDGET_USD`, off unless set, Paul's call 13 Nov 2026; `JobRunner.capReached`) leaves new jobs queued (`capBlocked`) once reached, tells Paul once per day, and `/resume` lifts it for the rest of that day; blocked jobs start when it is lifted or the next day begins. The cap counts the provider's own figure for the London day, not the sum of `costUsd` (described below); `costUsd` is still what a job records, and it is internal only. Commands outside that remit need a `YES <id>` reply. Recurring jobs run on cron. Models: `CHIEF_MODEL`, `WORKER_MODEL`, `ENGINEER_MODEL` (the maintainer uses it too) and `DESIGN_MODEL` all default to `haiku` (6 Nov 2026, Paul's call, in code, `fly.toml` and `.env.example`); override any one by env to put a role back on `sonnet` or `opus`. With `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` set, every agent runs through OpenRouter's Anthropic-compatible endpoint instead (`src/llm.ts`: `llmEnv` sets the SDK's base URL, bearer token, empty Anthropic key and every internal alias; `resolveModel` maps haiku, sonnet, opus and fable to `OPENROUTER_<ALIAS>_MODEL` or the defaults; the raw OpenRouter variables are stripped from worker environments), the executive assistant runs on Claude Sonnet on Anthropic's API directly whatever the default provider (Paul's call, 13 Nov 2026; `runRoute` in `src/llm.ts`, `RunOptions.provider`, `EA_MODEL` default `sonnet`; with no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` its runs fail as auth failures and never fall back), while its follow-up nudge drafts ("Draft nudge <id>") use `WORKER_MODEL` on the default provider, and failure messages name the provider in use. A run pinned to Anthropic strips every gateway variable from its environment (`DIRECT_STRIPPED`), however it got there. The chief's prompt carries `modelNote()`, the models actually in use, so it answers from that rather than from its checkout. One figure about money is ever shown, and it is the provider's (`src/spend.ts`; Paul's call, 13 Nov 2026, "Remove the agent sdk estimate it's not helpful"). The SDK's own `total_cost_usd`, priced from the rows `llmManagedSettings` hands the SDK as `modelPricing` (defaults in `DEFAULT_OPENROUTER_PRICES`, deliberately rounded up; `OPENROUTER_PRICES` adds or replaces rows; the start-up log names any model in use without one), is internal only: it is what a job's `costUsd` records, it drives one thing only, the Agent SDK's own per-run guard (`SDK_RUNAWAY_BUDGET_USD`, default 1000, far above an ordinary run's estimate: a runaway trip, never a job's cap), and it is the stand-in the daily cap is judged on when the provider cannot be read. A job's own cap (`JOB_BUDGET_USD`, `ENGINEER_BUDGET_USD`, `DEEP_RESEARCH_BUDGET_USD`, `DESIGN_BUDGET_USD`, all off by default) is counted instead in the provider's money, call by call (`src/realcost.ts`): each model reply's generation id is read from the SDK's message (`request_id`, the `gen-...` its own pricing endpoint takes, never the Anthropic-shaped `message.id`) and priced at `GET /api/v1/generation?id=`, which takes the same inference key, and the sum is what the cap is checked against, so a job is never stopped for budget while it has really spent pennies; a call the provider has not published yet (404) is read again in bounded passes rather than counted as unpriceable, an id it will not price at all (400) makes the figure a lower bound and is said so, the estimate is never substituted, and with no figure the cap simply does not fire. It is never shown, never labelled, and never called the bill, except on Anthropic directly (no OpenRouter key, and the ea's own runs), where there is no per-call price to read and the SDK's list-price cost is the bill: there the job's own cap is handed to the SDK and a stop is reported as Anthropic's own list price, not as an estimate. The real figure is the provider's own: on OpenRouter, `SpendMeter` reads `GET /api/v1/key` (the cumulative USD charged to the key the workforce runs on) at most once a minute, cached, with a 5s timeout, and keeps the day's starting reading in the store (`usageStart`) so today's spend is the difference and a restart does not reset it; a new London day re-bases from the last reading of the day before, a figure that goes backwards re-bases, and a reading older than 5 minutes is treated as stale. A restart that spans midnight re-bases the day on the first reading after boot, so spend between midnight and that reading is not counted; it is a soft cap, and a single run is still bounded by the per-run cap. `DAILY_BUDGET_USD` is compared against that figure, falling back to that internal stand-in only when OpenRouter cannot be read (a failure, a rate limit or a non-numeric reply, never as zero), and no message ever prints it: `spendDisplay` is the one way a spend figure reaches a message, and for the stand-in it reports that there is no figure, so `/spend` says the usage could not be read this time and shows no substitute and the once-a-day cap notice says the cap is being judged on an internal stand-in. A job is never held back on the internal stand-in alone while OpenRouter is reachable: `confirmCap` re-reads and releases the queue if the real spend is under the cap, and the watchdog reads the figure every minute and releases capped jobs. Anthropic mode is unchanged: no usage endpoint is read and the SDK's list-price cost is the figure, named as Anthropic's own list price rather than as an estimate, because there it is the bill. Nothing here needs a new secret: OpenRouter's usage endpoint takes the same key the models already use.

## Web search and browsing
`src/search.ts`: `web_search` (Brave, `BRAVE_API_KEY`) and `exa_search` (Exa, `EXA_API_KEY`), an in-process MCP server the service runs itself, so search works on any model and the keys never reach a model or a worker (`SEARCH_SECRETS` are stripped from every worker environment and scrubbed from errors); a tool exists only when its key is set; results are fenced as untrusted content. `src/browse.ts`: a browser (`open`, `read`, `click`, `type`, `back`, `sign_in`), one `BrowseSession` per job, closed when the job ends: headless Chromium spawned as the unprivileged designer user (never root; Playwright only connects over DevTools on loopback), a fresh profile, every request through the design worker's egress proxy (private and loopback addresses refused, so pages cannot reach the DevTools port), downloads, service workers, dialogs and new tabs refused, capped at `BROWSE_MAX_ACTIONS`. Reading (GET, HEAD, OPTIONS, `ALLOWED_METHODS`) works anywhere; any other request leaves only for a host where `SitePolicy.mayAct` says yes (`src/site-policy.ts`: `DEFAULT_ACT_SITES`, minus Paul's removals, plus his additions, stored as `siteRules` in the store and changed only through the chief's `site_allow` and `site_disallow`, each confirmed to Paul by the service; plus any vault domain). A click or Enter whose element, form buttons or form action match `CONSEQUENTIAL` (pay, buy, order, send, post, publish, delete, close account and the like) is refused on every site, and the agent reports what it would do. `NEVER_SITES` (money, email, Microsoft and work, password managers, government identity and tax, the workforce's own infrastructure) can never be allowed or stored, whatever is said in chat. `src/vault.ts`: Paul's logins, written on the one-time `/vault/add` page (link from the chief's `vault_add_link`, 15 minutes, single use, no-store and strict CSP headers, nothing echoed) and, the only other write path, by the sign-up gate when Paul approves an account (`Vault.save`, reachable from `src/signup-gate.ts` alone), stored AES-256-GCM under `DATA_DIR/vault` with `VAULT_KEY`, never shown to a model (`vault_list` shows domain, username and whether a two-factor key is stored); `sign_in` fills the form in the service only while the page host is the entry's domain over https, generates TOTP codes itself, refuses password fields to agents' `type`, and saves the signed-in cookies encrypted, restored per site in later sessions. Paul's call (13 Nov 2026): a site with a vault entry is signed in to and acted on freely. `src/web-tools.ts` decides who gets them: search for `SEARCH_KINDS` (research, write, ideate, advisor, prebid, cv) and the chief; the browser for `BROWSE_KINDS` (research, ideate, advisor, prebid); never ea, dns, engineering kinds or design.


## Live voice
`src/voice-bridge.ts`: when an allowlisted Discord user joins a voice channel the bot joins (intent `GuildVoiceStates`; after a restart it finds him if he is already in one), listens to that user only, records each turn until he pauses for `VOICE_SILENCE_MS` (`EndBehaviorType.AfterSilence`; prism-media and opusscript decode), drops turns that are too short or too quiet, transcribes the turn as a WAV with `transcribe` from `src/transcribe.ts` (OpenRouter, the voice-note call), and speaks Cleo's answer a sentence at a time (`SentenceSplitter`) through OpenRouter's `/audio/speech` (`speak`, `VOICE_TTS_MODEL`, `VOICE_TTS_VOICE`), decoded from MP3 by ffmpeg (in the image). Talking over her aborts the turn and stops playback. It leaves when he leaves or after `VOICE_IDLE_MINUTES`. Nothing is recorded or saved. `src/voice.ts`: Cleo (`VoiceBrain`), a fast OpenRouter model (`VOICE_MODEL`) with one tool, `ask_clearops`, which hands the request to the chief through `orchestrator.handle` as `voiceRequestText` ("Voice request from Paul ...", so no approval command can ever match) with the voice channel as `replyTo`; `VoiceTap` wraps the chief's and the jobs' messenger to catch the reply (spoken via `speakable`) or keep later messages as news, which the bridge announces at once when Paul is quiet. No GPU and nothing to warm up; on whenever `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` is set and `VOICE` is not `off`.

## Job outputs on GitHub
`src/publish-outputs.ts` (`OutputsPublisher`): every job that finishes `done` has its saved output copied, fire and forget (`JobRunner` `onOutput`), to the private repository `OUTPUTS_REPO` (default `drpaulfarrow/clear-outputs`, kept in its checkout under `DATA_DIR/repos`) into a folder by type (`src/output-folders.ts`: `designs/` for design kind and research titled as a design, spec or architecture, `research/`, `advice/` for advisor and prebid, `ideas/`, `engineering/`, `reviews/`, `ops/`) as `<folder>/<date>-<id>-<title>.md`, with `index.json` and a README index grouped by folder (date, id, title, kind, link, newest first). ea and cv outputs, outputs with no job record, and any research, write or analyse output whose brief or text names an uploaded file (`Connections.csv`, the uploads path) are never copied, and are removed from the tree if an earlier layout copied them (git history keeps them; the repository is private). The backfill reruns once per layout (`outputsBackfilled` holds `folders-v1`). It reads only `DATA_DIR/outputs`, never uploads or secrets, redacts secrets, and pushes only when GitHub says the repository is private at that moment (checked hourly, fails closed); otherwise Paul is told once what to change. Any failure is logged and swallowed: it never fails or delays a job or its message. A one-off backfill of every existing output runs at boot (`backfillOnce`, recorded in the `outputsBackfilled` setting). The service, not an agent, commits to that repository's default branch; it is a mirror, not code.

## The jobs board
`src/jobs-board.ts`: one read-only page Paul bookmarks on his phone instead of typing `/jobs` in chat. `/board` (whole message, handled by the orchestrator with no model) mints a long-lived token once, keeps it in the store (`settings.jobsBoardToken`) and replies with `${PUBLIC_URL}/jobs?t=<token>`; the token is compared in constant time, never logged, and only this page is behind it. `GET /jobs` renders the board (mobile-first, `meta refresh` every `BOARD_REFRESH_SECONDS`, 10) and `?format=json` returns the same data as JSON. It shows jobs only: running now (title, kind and repository in plain words, id, how long it has been running, when its worker last did anything), queued in order (its place on its repository's queue, the job it waits behind, or the hold after a credit or key failure, all from `JobRunner.isHeld` and `JobRunner.queuePosition`, so no queue logic is repeated here), finished in the last day (the outcome from `failureLabel`, and a link to the job's saved output in the private outputs repository when `src/output-folders.ts` says it is copied, or to the pull request that `job.pr` names) and each enabled schedule's next run (`Scheduler.upcoming()`). What it deliberately does not do: it never reads spend (not `src/spend.ts`, no `costUsd`, no cap figure), never renders a job's brief, output, transcript, file path, secret or anything from Paul's mail, calendar or uploads (an assistant output is never even read), and has no write path at all, so it cannot cancel, resume, retry, approve or merge anything (one GET route, no form, no model call). `test/jobs-board.test.ts` covers the token, the shape, the leaks and that running, queued and finished jobs render.

## GitHub access
`src/github-auth.ts`: with `GITHUB_APP_ID` and `GITHUB_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` (optional `GITHUB_APP_INSTALLATION_ID`, else found for `REPO_OWNER`) the service signs an RS256 JWT, exchanges it for an installation token, caches it until five minutes before expiry and re-mints automatically (`GithubAuth`); `GH_TOKEN` is the fallback when the App is not configured or cannot sign in. Workers get a fresh token per run (`workerGithubEnv`, called by `sdkRunner`): the token goes to an owner-only file under `DATA_DIR/secrets`, git's credential helper and a `gh` wrapper (`DATA_DIR/bin`, first on the worker's PATH) read it at call time, kept fresh for long jobs by `startTokenRefresher`; with the App active, `GH_TOKEN`, `GITHUB_TOKEN` and the App key are removed from the worker environment, and the key is stripped from every worker's environment. Tokens, JWTs and keys are never logged, saved or stored (`redact` covers them). Creating repositories always uses `GH_TOKEN`, never the App: GitHub does not let an App installation create repositories on a personal account, and the errors say so. The App must be installed on All repositories so new repositories are covered. Actions secrets are `GH_APP_ID`, `GH_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, `GH_APP_INSTALLATION_ID` (names cannot start with `GITHUB_`); `deploy.yml` maps them. `/status` (no model call) and the start-up log report the active auth and which managed repositories it reaches (`githubStatusLine`), and Paul is told once in plain English if the App fails or a managed repository is unreachable. Setup guide for Paul: `docs/GITHUB_APP.md`.

## Invariants (enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts`, which may be extended but never removed or weakened)
- Only senders in `ALLOWED_TELEGRAM_IDS`, `ALLOWED_DISCORD_IDS` or `ALLOWED_NUMBERS` get a reply (`isAllowed` in `src/config.ts`), and every listener checks before handling.
- WhatsApp webhook signatures are verified before any message is parsed.
- Attachments are downloaded only after the sender passes the allowlist. Uploads live only in the uploads directory on the data volume, never inside a git checkout, owner-only (0700/0600), with sanitised names and a size cap; they are never executed, and archives unpack into their own new directory. Every worker prompt carries `UPLOADS_RULE`.
- Every agent prompt carries the clean-room rule, including every kind added to `JOB_KINDS` later. Only engineering kinds (engineer, create_agent) get Edit, Write or unrestricted Bash.
- Only the maintainer merges. Engineers cannot merge or push the default branch; nobody force-pushes, rewrites shared history or deletes recursively. Branch protection on `main` (require a pull request and the `ci / test` check) is the hard backstop.
- The design worker gets only read-only tools plus its own `screenshot` and `save_asset` (its own folder only). The browser and branch previews run as the unprivileged `designer` user, never root, so they cannot read the service's environment, `DATA_DIR` (secrets included), the uploads or git credentials. A design job never gets the ea's Google server. Every request the browser makes, loopback included, goes through a per-capture egress proxy that refuses private addresses (checked on the parsed address, so IPv4 written as IPv6 is refused too), connects only to the address it checked, and allows only that capture's own local server. The design loop builds only through engineer jobs, is capped, and only a paused loop can be carried on.
- The executive assistant can send an email or a draft and create calendar invitations with attendees, and cancel events that have attendees (added 2 Nov 2026, notifying them), ONLY through the approval flow, a rule Paul deliberately widened on 2 Nov 2026 (it replaces "never send, never invite"); it can never delete, archive or relabel mail, delete other people's events, or answer invitations. `GoogleApi` in `src/google.ts` reaches only `ALLOWED_ENDPOINTS`, refuses any path segment that sends, trashes, modifies or moves, and accepts ordinary calendar writes only on the primary calendar, with `sendUpdates=none`, as marked holds with no attendees (personal holds need no approval). Personal holds and drafts are unchanged.
- The approval flow (`src/outbound.ts`, enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts` and `test/outbound.test.ts`): a job holding the `send` permit (granted by the chief only because Paul asked in chat; scheduled runs never get it) can only PREPARE a send (`prepare_send_draft` for a draft the assistant created, `prepare_send_email`) invite (`prepare_invite`) or cancel an event with attendees (`prepare_cancel_event`: the service reads the event itself, shows its title, time and the attendees who will be emailed, re-reads it before running and refuses holds, events Paul does not organise and events with nobody else invited; the DELETE endpoint in `APPROVED_ENDPOINTS` needs a grant and `sendUpdates=all`; it counts toward the daily cap; calendar.events already covers deletion so no re-consent). The service, not the model, shows Paul the exact recipients (to, cc, bcc), subject and body, or title, time, attendees, location and description, with an id. Only Paul's chat message `send <id>` (or `send it` when exactly one is waiting), parsed by the orchestrator after the allowlist check, before any model runs, and never from attachments, email, calendar, job output or uploads, approves it; `cancel <id>` cancels. Actions expire (24 hours), approval of one never authorises another, a draft is re-read and its hash compared with what Paul saw (changed means nothing is sent and a new approval is needed), drafts with attachments are refused, there is a daily cap (default 10 sends plus invites per London day) and a per-message recipient cap (default 10), every address must be well formed, text that looks like a secret or a private path is refused, and every action is logged in the store (approver, time, recipients, subject, Google's id). The send and invite endpoints (`APPROVED_ENDPOINTS`: `drafts/send`, `messages/send`, `calendars/primary/events` with attendees and `sendUpdates=all`) are reachable only through `GoogleApi.approved` with a single-use grant (`mintSendGrant`) bound to the exact request digest, minted only in `src/outbound.ts`; no other file mints grants, calls it or names a send endpoint, and `ALLOWED_ENDPOINTS` still has none. No scope change: gmail.compose and calendar.events already cover it. Scopes stay gmail.readonly, gmail.compose, calendar.readonly and calendar.events. Write tools exist in a job only with its permit, which the chief grants from Paul's chat and scheduled runs never get. The follow-up tools read Gmail and write only the workforce's own store. The service's hold release (`HoldWatcher`) runs no model, removes only holds recorded in a group placed by a job holding the holds permit, and still goes through `GoogleApi`, which re-checks each is a hold.
- Email and calendar content is untrusted: it reaches the model fenced with a per-job nonce, the ea prompt carries `EA_UNTRUSTED_RULE`, the ea kind has no WebFetch, and a draft may only go to addresses Paul named, people on the thread, or people he has written to. Google refresh tokens are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and owner-only under `DATA_DIR/secrets`, never in a checkout.
- The DNS agent changes records ONLY through the approval flow (`src/dns-gate.ts`, enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts` and `test/dns.test.ts`): prepare, show before and after, Paul's own `dns apply <id>`, snapshot, apply, log. Hard blocks are never overridable: deleting, NS, MX, SRV, CAA, SOA, email authentication records, apex A, AAAA and CNAME, wildcards, more than 5 records, domains off the allowlist, and every registrar action. Only `src/dns-gate.ts` mints DNS write grants or approves; no other file may.
- An account is created for Paul ONLY through the sign-up gate (`src/signup-gate.ts`, enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts` and `test/signup.test.ts`): a job holding the `signup` permit prepares, the service applies the rules and shows him the exact site, address and fields, and only his own `signup apply <id>` submits it. Hard blocks are never overridable: paying, card, bank and billing details, a paid trial that needs them, phone, identity, government and age verification, a money site, an email provider, Microsoft, a password manager, a government identity service or this workforce's own infrastructure, a site Paul did not allow, a second account where a login is stored, any change to an existing account and any post or publish as him. Nothing is ticked, no terms accepted, that Paul was not shown: a consent box the plan does not name stops the sign-up. The login reaches the vault only on a positive confirmation from the page, never on the absence of a complaint, and a submit that is not confirmed stores nothing. The browser it opens acts only on the plan's own domain, never on the wider act list, and carries only that domain's cookies. The generated password is never in chat, in a log or in a job output. Only `src/signup-gate.ts` approves a sign-up; no other file may.
- A purchase is placed ONLY through the purchase gate (`src/purchase.ts`, enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts` and `test/purchase.test.ts`): no agent presses the final order button (`FINAL_ORDER`) or types card details; nothing is pressed before Paul's own decision (his tap on the single-use approval page or `buy <id>`); expired, declined or changed purchases press nothing; only `src/purchase.ts` completes a purchase or reads the card, and the purchase record never holds the card.
- Agents run headless (`permissionMode: "dontAsk"`); every run has a turn limit. Money limits (per run and per day) are off unless their variables are set: Paul's call, 13 Nov 2026, "remove all payment caps".
- Every agent prompt carries Paul's messaging rule (`PAUL_STYLE` in `src/messages.ts`: three or four plain sentences, what was done, what he must do, a link; no headings, "Outcome:", bullets or tables), the chief's included, and every worker kind gets `REPORT_RULE` from `workerSpec()`, including kinds added later. A worker's answer is split by `splitReport`: the full report (verdict lines included) is the saved output; only the short plain message, made plain again by `plainMessage`, goes to chat.

## Rules for anyone (human or agent) working in this repo
