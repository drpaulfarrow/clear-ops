# clear-ops

The agentic workforce for Clear Exchange, operated by the founder from Telegram, Discord or WhatsApp, any or all at once.

## What this is
A small Node service. Messages arrive from Telegram (long polling, `src/telegram.ts`), Discord (gateway, `src/discord.ts`; every job Paul starts gets its own thread: under his message for the first job from it, otherwise a standalone thread in the text channel, including batches and jobs asked for inside a thread; follow-on jobs report in the original job's thread; threads auto-archive after a week and a report reopens one; a thread that fails to open is logged and the job reports in the conversation) or WhatsApp (`/webhook`), and replies go back to the conversation they came from through `src/channels.ts`. Jobs interrupted by a restart are re-queued on boot. Every job has a title (`src/titles.ts`): five words or fewer, chosen by the chief in `start_job` and `schedule_job` (validated), or derived for jobs the system starts ("Review: X", "Re-review 1: X", "Fix round 2: X", "Design round 2: X", "Build round 1: X", a scheduled run takes its schedule's title, "Draft nudge <id>"). The title names the Discord thread and is what every message, `/jobs`, `job_status` and queue line shows (`Fly deploy · engineer · 16d5d3`); the brief stays on the job unchanged for the worker. Jobs and schedules stored before titles get a derived one on load. a chief-of-staff agent (Claude Agent SDK) replies, and delegates longer work to worker agents (research, write, engineer, maintain, analyse, create_agent, ea, design, ideate, cv, dns, advisor, prebid) that run in the background and message the founder when done. Every agent can read every managed repository and the founder's uploads; every worker prompt names every checkout's local path (`repoPaths()` in `src/agents.ts`). Managed repositories (`src/repos.ts`, `repoRegistry`): `product` and `self` (PRODUCT_REPO, SELF_REPO; unchanged defaults and the targets of all older jobs), the `MANAGED_REPOS` list (default `site=drpaulfarrow/drpaulfarrowcom`, Paul's personal site, cloned under `DATA_DIR/repos/<alias>`), and repositories the workforce created, persisted in the store (`createdRepos`) and reloaded on boot. `Job.target` is a repository alias; `start_job` takes `repo` (alias, owner/name or Paul's name for it, default product) and `bootstrapRepos` (`src/bootstrap.ts`) clones each at start and fast-forwards an existing checkout to its default branch when it is on that branch with no local changes (`refreshCheckout`), so agents read the deployed code. Engineer and maintain work in the chosen checkout; research, write, analyse and design read it. A repository that cannot be cloned or created is reported to Paul once in plain English with exactly what to change in the token (`explainCloneFailure`, `explainCreateFailure`), and the token is redacted from every log and message (`redact`). New repositories: the `create_repo` permit (`src/repo-tools.ts`), which only an engineer job gets, only when the chief grants it because Paul asked in chat; it creates one private-by-default repository under `REPO_OWNER` with a README and initial commit (GitHub `auto_init`), registers and clones it, and nothing else (no deletion, visibility or settings change, no pushes). Research has a depth (`start_job` `depth`, or inferred from the brief by `detectDepth`): the saved report has no word cap and is sized to the brief, and a deep job writes a long, structured report with an executive summary, with higher turn and spend caps (`runLimits`, `DEEP_RESEARCH_BUDGET_USD`). Files he attaches on Telegram or Discord are saved by `src/uploads.ts` under `DATA_DIR/uploads` (`<date>-<name>`), confirmed in chat, and described to the chief in that turn (path, original name, type, size, caption); images are passed to the chief inline so it can see them. `/uploads`, `/delete upload <name>` and the chief's `list_uploads` and `delete_upload` manage them. The executive assistant (`ea`, `src/ea.ts`) works over Paul's Google Workspace at the level he approved, "drafts plus holds", widened on 2 Nov 2026 to sending and inviting with his per-item approval: it reads mail and every calendar, and only when the chief grants the job a permit from Paul's chat does it save Gmail drafts, place, move and remove holds on his own calendar, or (permit `send`) prepare an email or an invitation with attendees, which goes out only after Paul replies `send <id>` himself (`src/outbound.ts`, see the invariants). Paul connects once via a link from the chief (`/google/callback`); the refresh token is stored encrypted under `DATA_DIR/secrets`, and connecting schedules a weekday 07:30 brief and 18:00 wrap. The ea also keeps a follow-up list in the store (`src/followups.ts`: people waiting on Paul, his promises in sent mail, people he waits on, with due and stale dates), scanned in the morning brief, nudged in chat when stale, managed with `/followups`, `done <id>`, `snooze <id> <when>`, `draft nudge <id>`; drafts outreach with a Gmail link per draft; and proposes meetings (`propose_meeting`: two or three grouped holds plus Paul's booking link, `/booking`), and `HoldWatcher` in `src/meetings.ts` releases a group's holds once the meeting is confirmed on his calendar. Engineers implement on branches and open pull requests. One agent, the maintainer, reviews and merges; it runs automatically after every engineer job and every agent-creator job that may have opened a pull request. The agent-creator (`create_agent`) turns a plain-language request for a new agent into a recommended schedule on an existing kind or a new worker kind on a pull request against this repository. Only one engineering job (engineer or create_agent) runs per managed repository at a time (product, self, site and any other counted separately; `lockKey`); further ones queue in FIFO order in the store (`waitingFor`, `queuePos`) and start when the holder finishes, including across restarts. The maintainer and other kinds never queue. The review loop closes itself: when the maintainer's review of an engineer's or agent-creator's pull request ends `REVIEW <url>: changes requested`, the job runner starts an engineer fix round on the same pull request (queued like any engineering job, re-reviewed as usual), up to `MAX_FIX_ROUNDS` (3); after that, or on `REVIEW <url>: needs Paul: ...`, the founder is messaged once with what he must do. Engineers and the maintainer update branches from main with `gh pr update-branch` or `git pull --no-rebase origin main`, never by force. The design worker (`design`) screenshots the live site and branches with headless Chromium (`src/design.ts`), critiques them as buyer personas and writes brand work and specs; it writes no production code. The creative strategist (`ideate`) has only WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob and Grep (no Edit, Write, Bash or secrets), reads the saved outputs and both repositories, and writes a ranked list of improvement ideas with impact, effort and the cheapest experiment; it only lists options, and neither it nor the chief starts an engineer, design, analyse or create_agent job from its ideas until Paul picks one in chat. The pitch and strategy advisor (`advisor`, `src/decks.ts`) has WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob, Grep and one tool of its own, `save_deck_file`, which writes Markdown or text only (slide-ready Marp Markdown, one-pagers, consult notes) into `DATA_DIR/decks` (owner-only, outside every checkout); it builds publisher, AI-company and investor pitches in Paul's voice and acts as a candid consultant from the saved research, never invents traction, customers, quotes or numbers (sourced or marked ASSUMPTION, simulated results labelled), has no mail, send, Bash or job tools, never starts jobs, and is one-off. The Prebid expert (`prebid`) has only WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, Glob and Grep (no Edit, Write, Bash or secrets); it answers Prebid questions, designs and reviews the seller-side Prebid module, explains the Prebid codebases, contribution rules, release process and governance (CODEBASE mode, reading the forks under github.com/drpaulfarrow over the web where public) and watches releases from primary sources, states dates and versions, says "not found" rather than inventing fields or versions, never posts, files or messages anything, and starts no jobs. The CV writer (`cv`, `src/cv.ts`) has WebSearch, WebFetch (not LinkedIn), Read, Glob, Grep and one tool of its own, `save_cv_file`, which writes Markdown or text only into `DATA_DIR/cv` (owner-only, outside every checkout); it keeps a facts file, a master CV and bios, tailors them from facts Paul supplied, flags gaps as questions and possible Microsoft-confidential items for him, has no mail, send or job tools, and is one-off. The DNS agent (`dns`, `src/dns.ts`, `src/dns-gate.ts`, setup in `docs/GODADDY.md`) works over Paul's GoDaddy zones for the allowlisted `GODADDY_DOMAINS`: it has no Bash, Edit, Write or web tools, only its own `dns` server (read tools always; `dns_prepare_change` only for a job holding the `dns` permit, which the chief grants only because Paul asked in chat and scheduled runs never get). It only PREPARES: `DnsGate` reads the live zone, applies the hard blocks (no deleting, no NS, MX, SRV, CAA, SOA, SPF, DKIM, DMARC, apex A, AAAA or CNAME, no wildcards, at most 5 records, allowlisted domains only) and the service shows Paul the exact records before and after with an id; only his chat message `dns apply <id>` (whole message, id named, parsed by the orchestrator after the allowlist check and before any model; `dns cancel <id>`) applies it. Approvals expire (2 hours), a zone that changed since he saw it needs a new preparation, the whole zone is snapshotted before every applied change (`DATA_DIR/dns/snapshots`, owner-only) and every applied change is logged in the store and `dns/changes.log`. `GoDaddyClient.write` needs a single-use grant minted only in `src/dns-gate.ts`, and the client has no route to delete or to any registrar call (transfer, renew, contacts, nameservers). `GODADDY_API_TOKEN` (Bearer, Domains v3, wins when set) and `GODADDY_API_KEY` and `GODADDY_API_SECRET` (sso-key, v1 fallback) are service secrets, stripped from every worker's environment and scrubbed from errors and logs (`redact` too). The design loop (`start_job` with `loop: true`) closes itself the same way: a design report ending `DESIGN: spec ready` starts an engineer job on the product (queued and reviewed as usual); when the maintainer merges it, the next design round starts once the product's deploy is live; up to `MAX_DESIGN_ROUNDS` (3) implemented rounds, then a closing critique. `DESIGN: no high-impact issues` ends it early; `DESIGN: needs Paul: ...` pauses it, sends him the images, and `start_job` with `continueLoop` carries on. A worker run that ends in an API or SDK error rather than real work (no credit, a rejected key, rate limits or overload after retries, an execution error, an empty or error-only result; `runFailure` in `src/failures.ts`, fed by `RunResult.error` from `sdkRunner`, which reads the SDK's `is_error` results) is recorded as `failed` with `failure` and `error` set, never `done`; nothing follows it (no review, fix round, design step or "Finished"). `FailureNotifier` tells Paul in one plain message per burst (a short batch window, then one follow-up per cooldown, per reason). Credit and auth failures set the store's `queueHold`: no queued engineering job starts and new ones queue behind it, until `/resume`, the chief's `resume_jobs` or `retry <id>` (`JobRunner.resume` and `retry`) lifts it and reruns the failed jobs under the same ids. The chief's prompt says a failed job did no work, and names an active hold. Liveness (`src/jobs.ts`): every worker run has a heartbeat (`RunOptions.onActivity`, each SDK message; `lastActivityAt` and `startedAt` on the job) and an abort handle; `JobRunner.checkLiveness`, run every minute by `startWatchdog` once boot has resumed interrupted jobs, stops a job idle for `JOB_STALL_MINUTES` (30) or running past `JOB_MAX_RUNTIME_MINUTES` (240) as failed with failure `stalled` or `timeout`, stashes an engineering job's uncommitted work in its checkout (`stashWork`, recorded as `savedWork`, which a retry is told to apply), frees its lock so the next queued job starts, and tells Paul in one sentence; a worker that ignores the abort is left behind after a grace period. A job marked running with no live worker after boot is reclaimed like a restart (resumed, or stopped after three), and a free lock with waiters starts its queue. The chief's `job_alive` says whether a running job's worker is alive and when it last did anything, `cancel_job` (`JobRunner.cancel`, failure `cancelled`) stops it or takes it out of the queue and frees its slot, and `move_job_to_front` (`JobRunner.moveToFront`) reorders a repository's queue. A daily spend cap (`DAILY_BUDGET_USD`, default 20, summed over `costUsd` of jobs finished that Europe/London day; `JobRunner.capReached`) leaves new jobs queued (`capBlocked`) once reached, tells Paul once per day, and `/resume` lifts it for the rest of that day; blocked jobs start when it is lifted or the next day begins. Commands outside that remit need a `YES <id>` reply. Recurring jobs run on cron. Models: `CHIEF_MODEL`, `WORKER_MODEL`, `ENGINEER_MODEL` (the maintainer uses it too) and `DESIGN_MODEL` all default to `haiku` (6 Nov 2026, Paul's call, in code, `fly.toml` and `.env.example`); override any one by env to put a role back on `sonnet` or `opus`. With `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` set, every agent runs through OpenRouter's Anthropic-compatible endpoint instead (`src/llm.ts`: `llmEnv` sets the SDK's base URL, bearer token, empty Anthropic key and every internal alias; `resolveModel` maps haiku, sonnet, opus and fable to `OPENROUTER_<ALIAS>_MODEL` or the defaults; the raw OpenRouter variables are stripped from worker environments), the executive assistant runs on Claude Sonnet on Anthropic's API directly whatever the default provider (Paul's call, 13 Nov 2026; `runRoute` in `src/llm.ts`, `RunOptions.provider`, `EA_MODEL` default `sonnet`; with no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` its runs fail as auth failures and never fall back), while its follow-up nudge drafts ("Draft nudge <id>") use `WORKER_MODEL` on the default provider, and failure messages name the provider in use. A run pinned to Anthropic strips every gateway variable from its environment (`DIRECT_STRIPPED`), however it got there. The chief's prompt carries `modelNote()`, the models actually in use, so it answers from that rather than from its checkout. Cost is taken from the SDK's own `total_cost_usd`: on Anthropic at list prices, on OpenRouter at the price rows `llmManagedSettings` hands the SDK as `modelPricing` (defaults in `DEFAULT_OPENROUTER_PRICES`, rounded up; `OPENROUTER_PRICES` adds more; the start-up log names any model in use without one).

## Web search and browsing
`src/search.ts`: `web_search` (Brave, `BRAVE_API_KEY`) and `exa_search` (Exa, `EXA_API_KEY`), an in-process MCP server the service runs itself, so search works on any model and the keys never reach a model or a worker (`SEARCH_SECRETS` are stripped from every worker environment and scrubbed from errors); a tool exists only when its key is set; results are fenced as untrusted content. `src/browse.ts`: a read-only browser (`open`, `read`, `click`, `type`, `back`), one `BrowseSession` per job, closed when the job ends: headless Chromium spawned as the unprivileged designer user (never root; Playwright only connects over DevTools on loopback), a fresh profile, every request through the design worker's egress proxy (private and loopback addresses refused, so pages cannot reach the DevTools port), only GET, HEAD and OPTIONS requests leave the page (`ALLOWED_METHODS`), downloads, service workers, dialogs, new tabs and password fields refused, capped at `BROWSE_MAX_ACTIONS`. `src/web-tools.ts` decides who gets them: search for `SEARCH_KINDS` (research, write, ideate, advisor, prebid, cv) and the chief; the browser for `BROWSE_KINDS` (research, ideate, advisor, prebid); never ea, dns, engineering kinds or design.

## Job outputs on GitHub
`src/publish-outputs.ts` (`OutputsPublisher`): every job that finishes `done` has its saved output copied, fire and forget (`JobRunner` `onOutput`), to the private repository `OUTPUTS_REPO` (default `drpaulfarrow/clear-outputs`, kept in its checkout under `DATA_DIR/repos`) into a folder by type (`src/output-folders.ts`: `designs/` for design kind and research titled as a design, spec or architecture, `research/`, `advice/` for advisor and prebid, `ideas/`, `engineering/`, `reviews/`, `ops/`) as `<folder>/<date>-<id>-<title>.md`, with `index.json` and a README index grouped by folder (date, id, title, kind, link, newest first). ea and cv outputs, outputs with no job record, and any research, write or analyse output whose brief or text names an uploaded file (`Connections.csv`, the uploads path) are never copied, and are removed from the tree if an earlier layout copied them (git history keeps them; the repository is private). The backfill reruns once per layout (`outputsBackfilled` holds `folders-v1`). It reads only `DATA_DIR/outputs`, never uploads or secrets, redacts secrets, and pushes only when GitHub says the repository is private at that moment (checked hourly, fails closed); otherwise Paul is told once what to change. Any failure is logged and swallowed: it never fails or delays a job or its message. A one-off backfill of every existing output runs at boot (`backfillOnce`, recorded in the `outputsBackfilled` setting). The service, not an agent, commits to that repository's default branch; it is a mirror, not code.

## GitHub access
`src/github-auth.ts`: with `GITHUB_APP_ID` and `GITHUB_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` (optional `GITHUB_APP_INSTALLATION_ID`, else found for `REPO_OWNER`) the service signs an RS256 JWT, exchanges it for an installation token, caches it until five minutes before expiry and re-mints automatically (`GithubAuth`); `GH_TOKEN` is the fallback when the App is not configured or cannot sign in. Workers get a fresh token per run (`workerGithubEnv`, called by `sdkRunner`): the token goes to an owner-only file under `DATA_DIR/secrets`, git's credential helper and a `gh` wrapper (`DATA_DIR/bin`, first on the worker's PATH) read it at call time, kept fresh for long jobs by `startTokenRefresher`; with the App active, `GH_TOKEN`, `GITHUB_TOKEN` and the App key are removed from the worker environment, and the key is stripped from every worker's environment. Tokens, JWTs and keys are never logged, saved or stored (`redact` covers them). Creating repositories always uses `GH_TOKEN`, never the App: GitHub does not let an App installation create repositories on a personal account, and the errors say so. The App must be installed on All repositories so new repositories are covered. Actions secrets are `GH_APP_ID`, `GH_APP_PRIVATE_KEY`, `GH_APP_INSTALLATION_ID` (names cannot start with `GITHUB_`); `deploy.yml` maps them. `/status` (no model call) and the start-up log report the active auth and which managed repositories it reaches (`githubStatusLine`), and Paul is told once in plain English if the App fails or a managed repository is unreachable. Setup guide for Paul: `docs/GITHUB_APP.md`.

## Self-improvement
The engineer and maintainer may work on this repository (job target "self"). Nothing patches the running process: the maintainer merges to main, `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` runs the tests and deploys, and the Fly health check keeps the old machine serving if the new one fails. Definition of done: engineer, pull request open with green tests; maintainer, merged to main.

## Invariants (enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts`, which may be extended but never removed or weakened)
- Only senders in `ALLOWED_TELEGRAM_IDS`, `ALLOWED_DISCORD_IDS` or `ALLOWED_NUMBERS` get a reply (`isAllowed` in `src/config.ts`), and every listener checks before handling.
- WhatsApp webhook signatures are verified before any message is parsed.
- Attachments are downloaded only after the sender passes the allowlist. Uploads live only in the uploads directory on the data volume, never inside a git checkout, owner-only (0700/0600), with sanitised names and a size cap; they are never executed, and archives unpack into their own new directory. Every worker prompt carries `UPLOADS_RULE`.
- Every agent prompt carries the clean-room rule, including every kind added to `JOB_KINDS` later. Only engineering kinds (engineer, create_agent) get Edit, Write or unrestricted Bash.
- Only the maintainer merges. Engineers cannot merge or push the default branch; nobody force-pushes, rewrites shared history or deletes recursively. Branch protection on `main` (require a pull request and the `ci / test` check) is the hard backstop.
- The design worker gets only read-only tools plus its own `screenshot` and `save_asset` (its own folder only). The browser and branch previews run as the unprivileged `designer` user, never root, so they cannot read the service's environment, `DATA_DIR` (secrets included), the uploads or git credentials. A design job never gets the ea's Google server. Every request the browser makes, loopback included, goes through a per-capture egress proxy that refuses private addresses (checked on the parsed address, so IPv4 written as IPv6 is refused too), connects only to the address it checked, and allows only that capture's own local server. The design loop builds only through engineer jobs, is capped, and only a paused loop can be carried on.
- The executive assistant can send an email or a draft and create calendar invitations with attendees, and cancel events that have attendees (added 2 Nov 2026, notifying them), ONLY through the approval flow, a rule Paul deliberately widened on 2 Nov 2026 (it replaces "never send, never invite"); it can never delete, archive or relabel mail, delete other people's events, or answer invitations. `GoogleApi` in `src/google.ts` reaches only `ALLOWED_ENDPOINTS`, refuses any path segment that sends, trashes, modifies or moves, and accepts ordinary calendar writes only on the primary calendar, with `sendUpdates=none`, as marked holds with no attendees (personal holds need no approval). Personal holds and drafts are unchanged.
- The approval flow (`src/outbound.ts`, enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts` and `test/outbound.test.ts`): a job holding the `send` permit (granted by the chief only because Paul asked in chat; scheduled runs never get it) can only PREPARE a send (`prepare_send_draft` for a draft the assistant created, `prepare_send_email`) invite (`prepare_invite`) or cancel an event with attendees (`prepare_cancel_event`: the service reads the event itself, shows its title, time and the attendees who will be emailed, re-reads it before running and refuses holds, events Paul does not organise and events with nobody else invited; the DELETE endpoint in `APPROVED_ENDPOINTS` needs a grant and `sendUpdates=all`; it counts toward the daily cap; calendar.events already covers deletion so no re-consent). The service, not the model, shows Paul the exact recipients (to, cc, bcc), subject and body, or title, time, attendees, location and description, with an id. Only Paul's chat message `send <id>` (or `send it` when exactly one is waiting), parsed by the orchestrator after the allowlist check, before any model runs, and never from attachments, email, calendar, job output or uploads, approves it; `cancel <id>` cancels. Actions expire (24 hours), approval of one never authorises another, a draft is re-read and its hash compared with what Paul saw (changed means nothing is sent and a new approval is needed), drafts with attachments are refused, there is a daily cap (default 10 sends plus invites per London day) and a per-message recipient cap (default 10), every address must be well formed, text that looks like a secret or a private path is refused, and every action is logged in the store (approver, time, recipients, subject, Google's id). The send and invite endpoints (`APPROVED_ENDPOINTS`: `drafts/send`, `messages/send`, `calendars/primary/events` with attendees and `sendUpdates=all`) are reachable only through `GoogleApi.approved` with a single-use grant (`mintSendGrant`) bound to the exact request digest, minted only in `src/outbound.ts`; no other file mints grants, calls it or names a send endpoint, and `ALLOWED_ENDPOINTS` still has none. No scope change: gmail.compose and calendar.events already cover it. Scopes stay gmail.readonly, gmail.compose, calendar.readonly and calendar.events. Write tools exist in a job only with its permit, which the chief grants from Paul's chat and scheduled runs never get. The follow-up tools read Gmail and write only the workforce's own store. The service's hold release (`HoldWatcher`) runs no model, removes only holds recorded in a group placed by a job holding the holds permit, and still goes through `GoogleApi`, which re-checks each is a hold.
- Email and calendar content is untrusted: it reaches the model fenced with a per-job nonce, the ea prompt carries `EA_UNTRUSTED_RULE`, the ea kind has no WebFetch, and a draft may only go to addresses Paul named, people on the thread, or people he has written to. Google refresh tokens are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and owner-only under `DATA_DIR/secrets`, never in a checkout.
- The DNS agent changes records ONLY through the approval flow (`src/dns-gate.ts`, enforced by `test/invariants.test.ts` and `test/dns.test.ts`): prepare, show before and after, Paul's own `dns apply <id>`, snapshot, apply, log. Hard blocks are never overridable: deleting, NS, MX, SRV, CAA, SOA, email authentication records, apex A, AAAA and CNAME, wildcards, more than 5 records, domains off the allowlist, and every registrar action. Only `src/dns-gate.ts` mints DNS write grants or approves; no other file may.
- Agents run headless (`permissionMode: "dontAsk"`); spend is capped on every run.
- Every agent prompt carries Paul's messaging rule (`PAUL_STYLE` in `src/messages.ts`: three or four plain sentences, what was done, what he must do, a link; no headings, "Outcome:", bullets or tables), the chief's included, and every worker kind gets `REPORT_RULE` from `workerSpec()`, including kinds added later. A worker's answer is split by `splitReport`: the full report (verdict lines included) is the saved output; only the short plain message, made plain again by `plainMessage`, goes to chat.

## Rules for anyone (human or agent) working in this repo
- Clean room: never reference, copy or reconstruct anything from a repository called `comp-exchange` or "Atom", or any Microsoft internal material. Only public standards and general knowledge.
- Engineers open pull requests; the maintainer merges. Anything else that sends, posts, signs or pays goes through `propose_action` and an executor in `src/jobs.ts`.
- Only allowlisted senders are ever answered.
- Keep replies short; they are read on a phone. No em dashes, British English.
- Tests must pass: `npm test`. Typecheck: `npm run build`.

## Layout
- `src/index.ts` Express app and webhook. `buildApp()` takes injectable messenger, runner and store so tests run offline.
- `/spend [yesterday|YYYY-MM-DD]` is answered by the service with no model call: `JobRunner.spendOn` (also behind the daily cap) totals job costs for a London day; chief chat spend is not recorded.
- `src/status.ts` what the founder sees while the chief works: a typing indicator and one status line edited in place from the chief's tool calls (`describeToolUse`), removed when the reply lands. Falls back to one "Working on it." where a channel can neither edit nor type.
- `src/output-folders.ts` which folder an output goes in and which are never copied.
- `src/github-auth.ts` GitHub App JWT and installation tokens, caching, fallback to `GH_TOKEN`, worker environment, the `gh` wrapper, the health report.
- `src/repos.ts` the managed repository list, repository creation and the plain-English token failures. `src/repo-tools.ts` the `create_repo` tool and its permit note.
- `src/orchestrator.ts` chief-of-staff prompt, its tools (start_job, job_status, get_output, propose_action, schedule_job, list_schedules, cancel_schedule, remember), commands, approval handling.
- `src/agents.ts` Agent SDK runner and the worker specs.
- `src/jobs.ts` background job runner and the approved-action executors.
- `src/scheduler.ts` cron schedules.
- `src/store.ts` JSON state (sessions, jobs, actions, schedules, notes) under `DATA_DIR`.
- `src/telegram.ts` Bot API client and long-polling receiver.
- `src/discord.ts` gateway listener, messenger and job threads. `src/channels.ts` routes by address prefix.
- `src/design.ts` the design worker's screenshot and asset tools, URL guard, branch preview, the `DESIGN:` and `IMAGE:` report lines, and the wait for the product deploy.
- `src/google.ts` Google OAuth (consent link, PKCE, encrypted token storage, refresh, revoke) and `GoogleApi`, the one allowlisted door to Gmail and Calendar.
- `src/outbound.ts` the approval gate for sending mail, inviting people and cancelling events: prepare, preview, expiry, hash check, caps, grants, the chat command parser and the audit log in the store.
- `src/decks.ts` the advisor's `save_deck_file` tool and the private decks folder.
- `src/dns.ts` the GoDaddy client (reads, and the one grant-gated write path), the hard-block rules, snapshots and the change log. `src/dns-gate.ts` the DNS approval gate, the `dns apply <id>` parser and the dns worker's tools.
- `src/followups.ts` follow-up detection (requests, commitments, dates in plain words), the tracker and the stale nudge.
- `src/meetings.ts` slot picking for proposed meetings, the booking-link check and `HoldWatcher`, which releases holds once a meeting is confirmed.
- `src/messages.ts` how everything talks to Paul: `PAUL_STYLE`, the workers' two-part answer (`REPORT_RULE`: full report, then a short message between `<message_to_paul>` tags), `splitReport`, `plainMessage` for chat and `tidyReply` for the chief's replies.
- `src/failures.ts` failed runs: telling an API or SDK error from real work, the plain reason and what Paul does, and `FailureNotifier`, which batches failure messages.
- `src/titles.ts` job titles: validation, derivation from briefs, the titles of system-started jobs, display labels and the migration of untitled state.
- `src/llm.ts` the model provider: Anthropic by default, OpenRouter when `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` is set; alias mapping, the SDK environment and the price rows.
- `src/search.ts` Brave and Exa search. `src/browse.ts` the read-only browser. `src/web-tools.ts` which kinds get them.
- `src/london.ts` Europe/London date helpers.
- `src/ea.ts` the executive assistant's tools (mail search and threads, drafts, agenda, free slots, holds), the untrusted-content fence, the recipient guard and the default brief schedules.
- `src/whatsapp.ts` Cloud API client, inbound parsing, signature check, chunking.
- `src/uploads.ts` files the founder sends: download with a size cap, name sanitising, private storage, zip unpacking, listing and deletion, and what the chief is told.
